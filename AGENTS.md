## Story-first creative brief
Research the problem before celebrating technology. Use Person → Problem → Evidence → Frustration → Insight → Solution → Working Proof → Meaningful Outcome. Distinguish primary evidence from assumptions and illustrative scenarios from real customers. Preserve reproducibility and existing verified work. For infrastructure, start from a verified failure or engineering bottleneck when a character would be artificial. This convention is project-local; do not change unrelated repositories or global configuration.


## Hackathon demo and README presentation
Lead with the real-world problem and sponsor technology, then show the working product and independently verifiable evidence. Use concise, persuasive narration and real moving footage. Do not insert repetitive on-screen disclaimers such as "illustrative scenario", "genuine application footage", or "edited pacing is not a latency measurement" on every scene. Keep necessary factual labels (e.g. testnet, mock token, verified versus simulated) clearly and unobtrusively presented, and put detailed research limitations and editorial provenance in the README, evidence ledger, or submission notes. Never invent a paying client, real customer, measured performance, or on-chain outcome. Prefer one accurate, prominent disclosure over a distracting persistent footer.
